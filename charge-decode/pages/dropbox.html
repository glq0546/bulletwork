What is DROPBOX on my credit card statement?

Quick Answer: A charge from DROPBOX is an auto-renewing cloud storage subscription, usually the Plus, Professional, Family, or Business plan. Most people see it after a free trial ends or an annual plan renews. To stop it, cancel at dropbox.com/account/plan — deleting the app or ignoring the reminder email does not end the billing.

1. What is DROPBOX?

Dropbox is a cloud storage and file-sharing service launched in 2007 and headquartered in San Francisco. It keeps documents, photos, and project files synced across your phone, laptop, and browser, and it lets you share folders with clients or teammates. Paid plans renew automatically every month or every year, which is why the same amount can appear on your statement on a fixed schedule.

On a bank statement the charge usually reads DROPBOX, DROPBOX*, or DROPBOX.COM. Cards issued outside the United States sometimes show Dropbox International Unlimited Company instead, which is Dropbox's international billing entity. If you upgraded inside the iPhone or Android app, the charge may be filed under APPLE or GOOGLE with Dropbox in the description.

Merchant Code: DROPBOX / DROPBOX* / DROPBOX.COM

Billing Pattern: Monthly or annual auto-renewing subscription (individual, family, or business plan)

Recommended action: Sign in at dropbox.com and open Account → Plan to confirm which email address is being billed.

2. Why are you seeing this charge?

A free trial rolled into a paid plan

You started a Plus or Professional trial to send one large file, missed the end date, and the first real payment ran automatically.

An annual renewal you weren't tracking

Yearly plans bill once every twelve months, so a single large charge can appear out of nowhere long after you signed up.

A shared card or family plan

A spouse, roommate, or colleague added your card to a Dropbox Family or Business account that you never see day to day.

An in-app purchase through Apple or Google

If the upgrade happened on a phone, the bill may sit under APPLE or GOOGLE, and Dropbox support cannot cancel it for you.

An old or second account

Many people have both a personal and a work Dropbox login, and only one of them is the account they still actively use.

3. Is This a Scam or Legit?

⚠️ Dropbox is a real company — but verify the charge, not the email.

Dropbox is a publicly traded, well-established storage provider, so the charge itself is almost always a genuine subscription. What is not genuine are the phishing emails and texts that copy Dropbox branding and claim your payment failed. Those messages link to fake billing pages built to steal card numbers. Never update payment details from a link in an email; type dropbox.com into your browser instead. If the charge exists but you cannot find any matching account, treat it as unauthorized and contact your card issuer.

4. How to identify the account

Search your inbox for "Dropbox receipt" or "Your Dropbox invoice" — every successful payment generates an email to the address on the account.
Check work and personal addresses, plus any aliases, because the billing email is not always the login you remember.
If the statement shows APPLE or GOOGLE, open that store's subscription list first; the plan is managed there, not on dropbox.com.
Still stuck? Use "Forgot password" at dropbox.com with each email you own — a reset link only arrives for the account that actually exists.

6. How to cancel your Dropbox subscription

1. Sign in to the billed account

Go to dropbox.com and open Account → Plan (or dropbox.com/account/plan). The page shows the plan name, the renewal date, and the card on file.

2. Choose "Cancel plan"

Follow the prompts. Dropbox may offer a discount, a pause, or a smaller plan on the way out — decline if you genuinely want the billing to stop.

3. Confirm the end date

Cancellation normally takes effect at the end of the paid period. Your account reverts to Dropbox Basic, and you keep access until the renewal date shown.

4. Save the proof

Keep the confirmation email and screenshot the cancellation screen. If a charge appears afterwards, that record is what gets your money back.

5. Cancel store purchases in the store

Paid through the app? On iPhone open Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions; on Android open Google Play → Payments & subscriptions. Dropbox cannot cancel those for you.

6. On a team plan, ask the admin

Only a team admin can remove a license or downgrade the workspace, so contact whoever manages billing for the organization.

7. How to Get Your Money Back

Start with Dropbox billing support at dropbox.com/support: renewals that were canceled late or charged by mistake are often refunded as a courtesy when you ask promptly. If support declines, escalate to your card issuer. In the U.S., the Fair Credit Billing Act gives you 60 days from the statement date to dispute a billing error on a credit card.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dropbox a scam?

No. Dropbox is a legitimate cloud storage company founded in 2007 and used by millions of people and businesses. A DROPBOX charge on your statement is almost always a real subscription renewal. It can still be unauthorized if a family member signed up or an old account auto-renewed, so verify it in your account before disputing.

How do I cancel my Dropbox subscription?

Sign in at dropbox.com, open your account settings, and go to the Plan tab. Choose "Cancel plan" and follow the prompts; your account then drops to Dropbox Basic at the end of the billing period. If you pay through Apple or Google, cancel in that store's subscription settings instead.
