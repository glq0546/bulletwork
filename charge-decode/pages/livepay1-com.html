What is LIVEPAY1.COM on my credit card statement?

Quick Answer: A charge from LIVEPAY1.COM is a high-risk payment gateway used by hidden membership sites and adult-entertainment platforms. Small initial charges are often test transactions to verify your card is active before larger recurring fees begin. Because the descriptor is generic, the actual merchant is hidden. If you cannot trace the charge to a known account, freeze your card immediately and file a dispute for an unauthorized card-not-present transaction.

1. What is LIVEPAY1.COM?

Instead of a direct online store, LIVEPAY1.COM operates as a third-party payment gateway that processes transactions for other websites, many of which are membership-based portals or adult-entertainment platforms. Because the statement name shows the gateway rather than the specific service, the name on your bill won't match any obvious purchases. To track it down, you will need to compare the charge's exact date and dollar amount against your recent search history, email accounts, or trial signups.

3. Is this charge a scam or legitimate?

⚠️ High risk. Take action immediately.

Even if the charge is only a dollar or two, do not ignore it. Merchants or unauthorized parties frequently process a tiny test charge first to see if your card is active before draining larger amounts or starting an expensive subscription cycle.

While the payment gateway itself processes transactions legally, the specific business using it to charge your account may not have your consent. If you cannot trace this fee to a specific account, receipt, or family member, you should treat it as a compromised card and deal with it accordingly.

4. How to trace the charge back to an account

Run a targeted search in your email: Search all your email accounts for "livepay1", the specific transaction amount, or the date of the bill. Don't forget to check your spam and junk folders.
Check the status of the charge: Look closely at your online banking app to see if the transaction is "Pending" or "Posted." Pending charges can occasionally drop off, but if it posts to your account, the bank will need to step in.
Protect your info if you find nothing: If you find no matching account or receipt, avoid typing your sensitive card number into unrecognized help forms on the web. Instead, let your bank handle it directly.

6. How to stop future charges

Find the source account to cancel: If you did sign up for a service, closing the browser or deleting an app won't stop the fees. You must log into the specific membership portal, payment platform, or app store where the card was linked, and cancel the subscription directly.
Keep a clear paper trail: Document every step of your cancellation. Keep support tickets, take screenshots of success screens, and archive cancellation emails. This is your insurance if they try to charge you again.
Request a hard block from your bank: If they ignore your cancellation and keep billing, ask your bank to place a merchant stop-payment block on the descriptor. If your card was completely compromised, you'll need to order a new card and ask your bank to opt out of the automatic account updater program.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Is LIVEPAY1.COM a real company?

Not exactly—it acts as a generic billing gateway used by various subscription websites. The actual business charging you is likely a third-party service hidden behind this label, meaning the descriptor itself won't tell you what was purchased.

Should I contact LIVEPAY1.COM first?

Only if you remember making a purchase that might match this charge. If the charge is completely unfamiliar, it is safer to contact your bank immediately to secure your card, rather than sharing your personal or account information with an unrecognized portal.

Why are the charges small?

Scammers and high-risk subscription sites often bill in small amounts to avoid triggering fraud alerts. They may also be testing if your card is active before charging a larger monthly or weekly renewal fee.
