## Template 1: Unauthorized Charge Dispute

### When to Use
- You see a charge you did not authorize
- Someone else used your card without permission
- You never received the goods or services

### Who to Send It To
- Your card issuer's Billing Inquiries department. Use the Billing Inquiries address on your statement — NOT the payment address where you mail checks. Sending disputes to the wrong address may delay or void your FCBA rights.

### Expected Processing Time
- 30-45 days for investigation
- Provisional credit usually issued within 10 business days

---

[Your Name]
[Your Address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email]
[Date]

[Credit Card Company Name]
Billing Disputes Department
[Address from your statement]

RE: Unauthorized Charge Dispute
Account Number: [Your Card Number]
Date of Charge: [Date on Statement]
Amount: $[Amount]
Merchant: [Merchant Name as it appears]

Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to dispute a charge on my credit card statement that I did not authorize.

I noticed a charge of $[Amount] from [Merchant Name] on [Date]. I did not make this purchase, nor did I authorize anyone else to use my card for this transaction.

[Choose one:]
□ I have never done business with this merchant.
□ I cancelled my account with this merchant on [Date].
□ I returned the goods on [Date] and have attached proof of return.

I am requesting that this charge be removed from my account and that I receive a full refund. I have attached copies of my statement highlighting the disputed charge.

Please investigate this matter and provide me with written confirmation of the outcome. Under the Fair Credit Billing Act, I have the right to dispute billing errors, and I expect this to be resolved within 30 days.

Thank you for your prompt attention to this matter.

Sincerely,

[Your Signature]
[Your Printed Name]

Enclosures:
- Copy of credit card statement
- [Any supporting documents]

- 15-30 days if merchant cooperates

# 🎯 Module 5: Pro Tips for Maximizing Refund Success Rate

---

## Tip 1: Call at the Right Time

**Best times to call:**
- Tuesday through Thursday (avoid Monday and Friday)
- Early morning (8-10 AM local time) or late afternoon (4-6 PM)
- Avoid lunch hours (12-2 PM) and end of month (busy periods)

**Why it matters:** You'll get a more experienced representative who has more time to help you.

---

## Tip 2: Be Polite but Firm

**Do:**
- Stay calm and professional
- Clearly state what you want
- Use phrases like "I'd like to request a chargeback"
- Ask for a reference number

**Don't:**
- Yell or use profanity
- Accept the first "no"
- Hang up without getting a reference number

**Sample script:**
> "Hi, I'm calling about a charge on my statement that I'd like to dispute. The charge is from [Merchant] for $[Amount] on [Date]. I've already tried to resolve this with the merchant and [was unsuccessful / they haven't responded]. I'd like to initiate a chargeback."

---

## Tip 3: Know When to Say "Chargeback"

**Use the word "chargeback" when:**
- The merchant refuses to refund you
- You've tried to resolve it directly and failed
- The charge is clearly unauthorized

**Why it matters:** "Chargeback" is a specific legal process that banks take more seriously than a general "dispute." It triggers a formal investigation.

---

## Tip 4: Use Your Bank's App

**Many banks now offer:**
- One-tap dispute buttons in their mobile apps
- Automatic provisional credit while investigating
- Real-time status updates

**Banks with good dispute apps:**
- Chase Mobile
- Bank of America Mobile
- Capital One Mobile
- Amex Mobile

**Pro tip:** App-based disputes are often processed faster than phone disputes.

---

## Tip 5: Document Everything

**Keep records of:**
- Screenshots of the charge on your statement
- Emails with the merchant
- Chat transcripts
- Phone call dates, times, and representative names
- Reference numbers for disputes
- Cancellation confirmations

**How to organize:**
- Create a folder on your computer or cloud storage
- Name files with dates: "2026-06-09_MerchantName_Dispute"
- Keep everything for at least 12 months after resolution

---

## Tip 6: Escalate Strategically

**If your dispute is denied:**

1. **Ask for the reason in writing** — They are legally required to provide this
2. **Submit new evidence** — Anything you missed the first time
3. **Request a supervisor** — Sometimes a fresh look helps
4. **File a CFPB complaint** — consumerfinance.gov/complaint
5. **Contact your state AG** — Especially for larger amounts

**CFPB Complaint Template:**
> "I filed a dispute with [Bank] on [Date] for a charge from [Merchant]. The dispute was denied on [Date], but I believe this decision is incorrect because [reason]. I have attached supporting documentation. Please investigate this matter."

---

## Tip 7: Leverage Social Media

**When to use social media:**
- The merchant is ignoring your emails
- You've been waiting more than 30 days
- You want to speed up the process

**How to do it effectively:**
- Tweet at the company's official account
- Keep it factual and professional
- Include your case/reference number
- Don't share personal information publicly

**Example tweet:**
> "@CompanyName I've been trying to resolve a billing issue for 30+ days (Case #12345). Can someone please help? I've emailed support@company multiple times with no response."

---

## Tip 8: Know Your Legal Rights

**Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) protects you:**
- You have 60 days from the statement date to dispute
- Banks must investigate within 30 days (45 days for some cases)
- You receive provisional credit during investigation
- Banks must explain their decision in writing

**Your rights include:**
- Right to dispute billing errors
- Right to withhold payment for disputed charges
- Right to receive a written explanation
- Right to escalate to CFPB
> **⚠️ Important:** While calling your bank is fast, only a written dispute mailed to the billing inquiries address within 60 days of the statement date legally preserves your rights under the Fair Credit Billing Act.
